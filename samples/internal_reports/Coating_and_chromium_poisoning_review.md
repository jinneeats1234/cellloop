# Protective coatings and chromium poisoning — review memo (internal)

Fictional data for demonstration.

## Background

Ferritic stainless supports (430L, Crofer 22 APU) release volatile chromium species at operating temperature. Chromium deposits at the LSCF cathode triple-phase boundary and raises polarization resistance over time.

## Findings

Uncoated cells showed degradation rates of 3 to 4.5 %/kh at 650 °C. MnCo2O4 spinel coatings of 5 to 10 µm reduced degradation to below 2 %/kh and lowered initial polarization ASR by about 0.08 Ω·cm². Coatings thicker than 12 µm added measurable ohmic resistance (about 0.003 Ω·cm² per µm) and showed cracking after 50 thermal cycles. CeO2 coatings were easier to apply but less effective at blocking chromium than spinel.

## Recommendation

Use MnCo2O4 spinel at 6 to 9 µm as the baseline coating. Re-test CeO2 only if spinel processing cost becomes a manufacturing blocker.
